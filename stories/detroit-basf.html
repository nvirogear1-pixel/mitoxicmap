BASF Wyandotte — 40 Years

A chemical plant has been pouring toxic waste into the Detroit River for 40 years.

BASF's 230-acre North Works facility in Wyandotte has been manufacturing chemicals since the 1800s. Groundwater contaminated with PFAS, mercury, arsenic, benzene, and naphthalene has been flowing into the Detroit River at up to 60 gallons per minute.

The contamination enters the river roughly 1,700 feet from Wyandotte's municipal drinking water intake.

60 gal/min
Contaminated groundwater into Detroit River
Documented Fact — EPA + Planet Detroit
First consent agreement: 1980. EPA consent: 1994. EGLE ordered BASF to stop in March 2025. EPA approved cleanup plan June 30, 2026. Construction begins early 2027.
planetdetroit.org | epa.gov
The Drinking Water Intake

1,700 feet from someone's drinking water.

The contaminated groundwater seeps into the Detroit River roughly 1,700 feet from Wyandotte's municipal drinking water intake. The state reviewed this in 2022 and determined it was "unlikely to cause a public health impact that requires immediate attention."

Some of the groundwater has had a pH as high as 13.22. EPA classifies anything above 12.5 as hazardous waste.

Documented Fact — EGLE 2022 Review
"The location of the drinking water intake and quantity of river water mixing with BASF's pollution meant it was unlikely to cause a public health impact that requires immediate attention."
Planet Detroit, June 30, 2026
EPA Policy Change — January 2026

The EPA decided it will no longer count how many deaths air pollution rules prevent.

On January 9, 2026, the EPA announced it will stop calculating monetary benefits of deaths prevented and health care costs saved when considering air pollution rules for PM 2.5 and ozone. The agency will still consider costs to industry.

Wayne County already fails to meet federal PM 2.5 standards. A federal appeals court returned Detroit to ozone nonattainment in December 2025. The American Lung Association gave Detroit an "F" rating.

Documented Fact — EPA Decision + EGLE + ALA
Nick Leonard, Great Lakes Environmental Law Center: "Regulating fine particulate matter and ozone is massively important for Detroit."
Planet Detroit, Jan 15, 2026 | EPA Green Book
Detroit River Cleanup — $10 Million

The Detroit River is a designated Area of Concern. It's loaded with toxic compounds.

PCBs. Dioxins. Cyanide. Heavy metals. PFAS. The Detroit River has been a federal Area of Concern since 1987.

The EPA and EGLE have already addressed 400,000 cubic yards of contaminated sediment and restored 320+ acres of habitat. In June 2026, they announced $10 million in new funding — EPA $6.5M + EGLE $3.5M.

A new Superfund site was added for the Trenton Channel — contaminated with PCBs, cyanide, and metals.

Documented Fact — EPA + EGLE Press Releases
Five additional remedial investigations scheduled for 2026.
EPA Detroit River AOC | EGLE Press Release | EPA Press Release
Lead in the Water

33 communities had a lead reading above 12 ppb.

From 2018 to 2025, 33 Great Lakes Water Authority communities in southeast Michigan reported a lead 90th-percentile above 12 ppb. 25 had an official action level exceedance. The state action level was lowered to 12 ppb in January 2025.

Detroit has replaced more than 16,000 lead service lines. Flint completed its lead pipe replacement in 2026 after a decade-long legal battle — nearly 11,000 pipes replaced.

Documented Fact — Planet Detroit + City of Detroit
Detroit water sampling stayed under the Lead and Copper Rule action level in Summer 2025. Wayne has removed all known lead service lines as of 2025.
Planet Detroit, June 2026 | Wayne County
PFAS Everywhere

98% of surface water sites nationally tested positive for PFAS.

The Waterkeeper Alliance tested surface water across 19 states (Phase II, 2025). 98% of sampling sites had one or more types of PFAS. Phase 1 in 2022 found 83%. In metro Detroit specifically, 15 public water systems may be at risk of exceeding federal guidelines.

The Detroit wastewater treatment plant has discharged PFAS into the river for decades — within state and federal permit limits.

Documented Fact — Waterkeeper Alliance + WDET
PFAS detected in multiple metro Detroit water systems. The chemicals are in the river, in the drinking water, and the rules are being weakened, not strengthened.
Waterkeeper Alliance | Planet Detroit, June 2024
