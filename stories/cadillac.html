1983 — The Discovery

The EPA walked into a small plating shop in Cadillac, Michigan.

What they found would change the town forever.

5,400 gal
Waste cyanide on-site

Plus 140 barrels of waste cyanide mix. Plus 3,450 gallons of acid. Plus 5,000 gallons of other hazardous waste. All sitting on the ground at Northernaire Plating Company, 1002 6th Street, Cadillac.

Documented Fact — Court Record
US v. Northernaire Plating Co., 670 F. Supp. 742 (W.D. Mich. 1987): "Included among the substances found at the Northernaire site were 5400 gallons of waste cyanide, 140 barrels of waste cyanide mix, 3450 gallons of acid and 5000 gallons of [other waste]."
The Spread

The cyanide didn't stay in the soil.

Northernaire Plating was a custom chromium and nickel electroplater. They used cyanide-based solutions to plate metal. When they were done, they poured waste into the sewer and dumped it on the ground.

The cyanide leached down. Into the groundwater. Into the aquifer that flows beneath Cadillac.

Documented Fact — EPA Record of Decision
EPA ROD, Northernaire Plating, MI: "The Northernaire Plating facility provided custom chromium and nickel [plating]... cyanide) to the soil which have subsequently leached from the soil to the [groundwater]."
The Commingling

Two sites became one problem.

Next door, in the Cadillac Industrial Park, KYSOR Industrial Corporation had been making automotive parts since 1959. Their waste disposal created six separate groundwater plumes of volatile organic compounds.

When Northernaire's cyanide and chromium plume reached KYSOR's property, the plumes merged. Six became seven. All flowing north.

Documented Fact — EPA Record of Decision
EPA ROD, Kysor Industrial, MI: "contamination by hexavalent chromium and cyanide." The Kysor site has "six commingled groundwater contaminant plumes."
The Migration

The plume reached a residential area.

Contaminated groundwater migrated under the city of Cadillac and into Haring Township. Into a residential area. Where people have private wells. Where families drink from the aquifer.

The City of Cadillac monitors 42 wells — 21 shallow, 21 intermediate — to track the commingled plumes. Shallow wells are 35-70 feet deep. Intermediate wells are 120-200 feet deep.

Documented Fact — EPA Five-Year Review
EPA Fifth Five-Year Review (2020): Groundwater migrated northward to Haring Township. City adopted new groundwater use restriction ordinance September 2018. Haring Township restrictions also strengthened.
The Pump That Never Stops

The cleanup system is still running. Right now.

EPA placed Northernaire on the National Priorities List in 1985. KYSOR followed in 1989. A pump-and-treat system was installed to pull contaminated water out of the ground, clean it, and return it.

Soil removal was completed by 1996. Soil vapor extraction shut down in 2010. But the groundwater pump-and-treat system is still running. In 2025. Forty-two years after the cyanide was found.

EPA's next five-year review is planned for 2025. Fall 2025: EPA and the Cadillac Local Development Finance Authority will sample monitoring wells for VOCs, hexavalent chromium, and PFAS.

Documented Fact — EPA Site Status
KYSOR Industrial Corp Superfund Site (EPA ID: 0502480): "Operations and maintenance activities are ongoing for the groundwater remediation system."
2024-2025 — The New Threat

On top of the cyanide, they found PFAS.

In late 2024 and early 2025, Michigan's PFAS Action Response Team (MPART) sampled residential wells near the Cadillac Industrial Park. Results: PFAS above Michigan Drinking Water Criteria in some private wells.

The local health department offered filters to affected residents. EPA data indicates the KYSOR and Northernaire Superfund sites are not the source of the PFAS. The source is still under investigation.

Documented Fact — MPART/EGLE
Michigan PFAS Response: "PFAS is present above Michigan Drinking Water Criteria in some private wells in the Cadillac Industrial Sampling Area."

ATSDR Health Assessment (1989)

The CDC's own assessment said: this is a health risk.

In 1989, the Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry — the CDC's environmental health arm — conducted a formal Public Health Assessment of the Northernaire Plating site.

They found the site was "approximately 1,500 feet from the Cadillac municipal well field." Two chromium plumes in the groundwater. Widespread VOC contamination.

Their words: "The use of the VOC or metal-contaminated ground water for potable purposes could pose a significant health risk."

Also: "Inhalation exposure to dust or the ingestion of soil from these contaminated areas could pose a potential health risk."

Documented Fact — ATSDR Federal Health Assessment (1989)
OSTI ID: 5219404. Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry. CERCLIS No. MID020883609.
The Second Layer — PBBs (1973)

It's not just cyanide. PBBs were dumped in a Cadillac landfill too.

In 1973, the Michigan Chemical Corporation accidentally shipped polybrominated biphenyls (PBBs) instead of cattle feed supplement. It was the largest food contamination event in U.S. history. Millions of pounds of contaminated material needed disposal.

ATSDR's own Toxicological Profile for PBBs documents: the waste "were disposed of in a sanitary landfill in Cadillac, Michigan."

PBB health effects documented by ATSDR and MDHHS: skin problems, altered thyroid function, altered liver function, breast cancer risk, miscarriages, multigenerational effects.

Documented Fact — ATSDR Toxicological Profile for PBBs
atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp68.pdf — PBB screenings were held in Cadillac as part of the PBB Oral History Project.

What Nvirogear Does NOT Claim

Concurrent documentation. Not causation.

Nvirogear documents what federal and state health agencies have published. We do not claim that contamination caused any specific person's illness.

What we can document: Three distinct contamination layers exist in Cadillac — cyanide from plating (1971), PBBs from Velsicol (1973), and PFAS (2024). Wexford County's cancer rate is above the state average. The CDC assessed health risks at the Northernaire site in 1989.

What we cannot claim: That any specific contamination caused any specific cancer case. That the elevated cancer rate is caused by the Superfund sites. That PBB disposal, cyanide, and PFAS are connected beyond geography.
