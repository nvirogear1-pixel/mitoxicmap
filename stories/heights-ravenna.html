The Corridor

481 parcels. One road. A contamination source hiding in plain sight.

Heights-Ravenna Road runs through Fruitport Township in Muskegon County, Michigan. Along this road are 481 parcels of land — homes, businesses, farms, and vacant lots. Their combined State Equalized Value is $64.7 million. Surrounding them are 37 confirmed environmental contamination sites. At the center of it all is a former laundromat at 3523 Heights-Ravenna Road — a site the State of Michigan has confirmed as contaminated with PFAS at levels nearly 14 times the drinking water standard.

This is not an investment report. This is a community intelligence document. It exists because the people who live on this road deserve to know what the public records say about the ground beneath their homes.

The Source: 3523 Heights-Ravenna

A laundromat. 1981 to 2005. PFOS at 221 parts per trillion. The drinking water limit is 16.

The Baseline Environmental Assessment (BEA) is a legal mechanism under Michigan's Part 201. It allows a new owner to purchase contaminated property without inheriting cleanup liability — as long as they don't make the contamination worse. The contamination stays in the ground. The building goes up above it.

The Wellogic database shows 34 water wells along this corridor. The shallowest static water level recorded is 2 feet below ground. That means surface contamination can reach drinking water almost immediately. The lithology is dominated by sand layers — 79 of 143 layers are sand — which means groundwater flows fast in this area.

If you're on a private well within 0.5 miles of 3523 Heights-Ravenna: Michigan's free well testing program does NOT include PFAS. You need a state-certified lab. Contact Muskegon County Health Department: 231-724-6208.
